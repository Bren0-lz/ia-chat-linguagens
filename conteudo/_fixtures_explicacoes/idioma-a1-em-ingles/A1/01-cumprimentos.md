<b>WHAT IT IS AND WHEN TO USE IT</b>
Greetings and goodbyes are the words that open and close a conversation in English. You use them every day, when you meet someone and when you leave.

<b>THE FORMS</b>
<i>To greet someone:</i>
- <b>Hello</b> and <b>Hi</b> (Hi is more informal)
- <b>Good morning</b> (until midday)
- <b>Good afternoon</b> (from midday to the end of the day)
- <b>Good evening</b> (when you arrive at night)

<i>To ask how someone is:</i>
- <b>How are you?</b>

<i>To answer:</i>
- <b>I'm fine, thank you.</b>
- <b>I'm good.</b>
- <b>Not bad.</b>

<i>To say goodbye:</i>
- <b>Goodbye</b> and <b>Bye</b>
- <b>Good night</b> (when you leave at night, or before sleeping)
- <b>See you</b>, <b>See you later</b>, <b>See you tomorrow</b>

<i>Polite words:</i>
- <b>Please</b>, <b>Thank you</b>, <b>Thanks</b>, <b>You're welcome</b>

<b>EXAMPLES</b>
- Hi, John! How are you?
- Good afternoon. A coffee, please.
- I'm going home now. See you tomorrow!
- Thank you for your help. Good night!

<b>COMMON MISTAKES</b>
The most common mistake is to greet someone with <i>Good night</i> when you arrive. Good night is only for leaving.
wrong: "Good night! A table for two, please."
the correct form is: "Good evening! A table for two, please."

<b>SUMMARY</b>
Good evening when you arrive at night, and Good night when you leave.
