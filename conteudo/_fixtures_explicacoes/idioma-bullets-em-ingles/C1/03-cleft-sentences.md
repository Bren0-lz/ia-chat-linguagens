<b>O QUE É E QUANDO USAR</b>
Veja abaixo.

<b>AS FORMAS</b>
- Cleft sentences are structures that split a sentence to put emphasis on one specific part of it.
- We use them above all in speech, when we want to highlight the information that we take to be new.
- There are two main types, and you can see all of them here, with the forms that each one takes.
- The it-cleft puts the focus on the element that comes right after it is or it was in the sentence.
- It is or it was plus a person is followed by who or that, and then by the rest of the sentence.
- It is or it was plus a thing is followed by that or which, and then by the rest of the sentence.
- It is or it was plus a place is followed by where, and it is or it was plus a time by when.
- The wh-cleft puts the focus on the element that comes after the verb to be in the sentence.
- What plus the subject plus the verb plus is or was is followed by the focused information.
- All plus the subject plus the verb plus is or was is also followed by the focused information.

<b>EXEMPLOS</b>
Veja abaixo.
- My sister broke the vase, and this is the plain sentence that we are going to work with here.
- It was my sister who broke the vase, and here the emphasis falls on the person who did it.
- I need a new car, and what I need is a new car, where the emphasis falls on what is needed.
- He complained about the noise, and it was the noise that he complained about, with the focus there.

<b>ERROS COMUNS</b>
Veja abaixo.
- The main mistake here is the agreement of the verb that comes after who or that in the it-cleft.
- That verb has to agree with the subject in focus, and it never agrees with the word it.
- wrong: It is I who is responsible for the project, because the verb does not agree there.
- the correct form is: It is I who am responsible for the project, and now the verb agrees.

<b>RESUMO</b>
Use it is that or what is to split a sentence and to put the focus on one piece of information.
