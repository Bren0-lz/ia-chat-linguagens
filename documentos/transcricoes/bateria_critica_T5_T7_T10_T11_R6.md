- What it is and when to use:  
  The past continuous shows an action that was happening at a specific time in the past.  
  Use it for a long action that gets interrupted by a short past‑simple action, often with WHEN or WHILE.  
- Forms / rules (complete):  
  Positive: was / were + verb‑ING (I was cooking, they were playing).  
  Negative: wasn't / weren't + verb‑ING (He wasn't sleeping).  
  Question: Was / Were + subject + verb‑ING? (Were you working?)  
- Examples:  
  I was watching TV when the lights went out.  
  While she was studying, he arrived late.  
  They weren't listening while the teacher explained.  
- Common errors to avoid:  
  Using past continuous for a quick action (use past simple instead).  
  Forgetting the –ING ending.  
  Mixing up which action is longer; the continuous must be the longer one.  
- One‑line summary:  
  Past continuous = was/were + verb‑ING for a longer past action interrupted by a short past‑simple event.
