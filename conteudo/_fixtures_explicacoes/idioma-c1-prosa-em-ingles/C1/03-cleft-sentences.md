<b>WHAT IT IS AND WHEN TO USE IT</b>
<i>Cleft sentences</i> are structures that "split" a sentence to put emphasis on one specific part. We use them, above all in speech, to highlight the piece of information that we take to be new, surprising or the most important one in the context, and so we steer the attention of the listener to it.

<b>THE FORMS</b>
There are two main types, and you can see all of them here:

<i><b>It-Clefts</b></i> (the focus falls on the element that comes right after <i>it is/was</i>)
- <i>It is/was + [person] + <b>who/that</b> + ...</i>
- <i>It is/was + [thing/idea] + <b>that/which</b> + ...</i>
- <i>It is/was + [place] + <b>where</b> + ...</i>
- <i>It is/was + [time] + <b>when</b> + ...</i>
- <i>It is/was + [reason] + <b>why</b> + ...</i>

<i><b>Wh-Clefts</b></i> (the focus falls on the element that comes after the verb to be)
- <i><b>What</b> + [subject] + [verb] + <b>is/was</b> + [focused information]</i>
- <i><b>All</b> + [subject] + [verb] + <b>is/was</b> + [focused information]</i>
- <i>Note: in the structure "What [someone] did was...", the verb that follows can come with or without "to". Ex: What he did was (to) leave.</i>

<b>EXAMPLES</b>
- Plain sentence: <i>My sister broke the vase.</i>
- Emphasis on the person: <i><b>It was my sister who</b> broke the vase.</i>

- Plain sentence: <i>I need a new car.</i>
- Emphasis on the need: <i><b>What I need is</b> a new car.</i>

- Plain sentence: <i>He complained about the noise.</i>
- Emphasis on the object of the complaint: <i><b>It was the noise that</b> he complained about.</i>

- Plain sentence: <i>She just called her mother.</i>
- Emphasis on the action: <i><b>What she did was</b> call her mother.</i>

<b>COMMON MISTAKES</b>
The main mistake here is the agreement of the verb that comes after <i>who/that</i> in the <i>It-cleft</i>. That verb has to agree with the subject in focus, and never with <i>it</i>.

wrong: <i>It is I who <b>is</b> responsible for the project.</i>
the correct form is: <i>It is I who <b>am</b> responsible for the project.</i>

wrong: <i>It was the employees who <b>was</b> asking for a raise.</i>
the correct form is: <i>It was the employees who <b>were</b> asking for a raise.</i>

<b>SUMMARY</b>
Use <i>It is... that...</i> or <i>What... is...</i> to split a sentence and to put the focus on one specific piece of information.
